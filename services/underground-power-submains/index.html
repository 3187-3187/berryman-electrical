What we do

Electrical work we can help with.

Each installation or fault is assessed according to its condition, equipment and required outcome.

Power to sheds and outbuildings

Private electrical feeds for workshops, garages and separate buildings, planned against the equipment to be used.

Submains and secondary switchboards

Cabling and distribution from an existing board to a sub-board, considering suitable isolation and circuit protection.

Underground conduit and pits

Assess existing conduits and pits, or plan new cable-pulling routes with workable bends and access.

Long cable runs

Consider voltage drop, cable capacity, installation conditions and future loads instead of selecting cable by distance alone.

Faults on buried supplies

Investigate an existing underground electrical fault and determine whether safe repair or replacement is appropriate.

Staged site upgrades

Coordinate power routes with building work, new sheds, landscaping, earthworks and future requirements.

Getting the scope right

What we consider before starting.

Check for buried services

Existing underground utilities and other assets must be identified appropriately before excavation.

Review source supply

The switchboard, supply capacity, earthing, protection and destination loads all affect the design.

Agree trenching and access

Clarify excavation responsibility, conduit requirements, pits, reinstatement and safe access before work begins.

Confirm network boundaries

Distributor assets, services and metering may require approvals or an accredited service provider, separate from private submains.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions.

Can you install underground power to a workshop?

Yes, subject to site assessment, the supply available and the proposed equipment loads.

Can the existing conduit be reused?

Sometimes. Its size, route, condition and installation suitability must be assessed first.

Do you arrange trenching?

The trenching arrangements can be discussed and coordinated as part of the job scope, including other contractors if needed.

Does this include a new supply from the street?

Not automatically. New network connections and certain service-side tasks require separate scope, approvals or accreditation.

What helps you prepare a quote?

Send the suburb, approximate distance, destination equipment, proposed route and safe photos of existing boards or conduits.
